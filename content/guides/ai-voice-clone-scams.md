The phone rings. It's your son, your mom, your partner. They sound terrified. They've been in an accident, or arrested, or they're stranded, and they need money **right now**. And they're begging you not to tell anyone.

Except it isn't them. It's a scammer using an **AI clone of their voice**.

This is one of the fastest-growing scams of the AI era, and it works because it hijacks the one thing you'd never question: a loved one's voice. The good news is that a few simple habits shut it down completely.

## How the scam works

1. **They get a voice sample.** A short clip is often enough: a social media video, a podcast, even a voicemail greeting.
2. **They clone it.** Widely available AI voice tools can turn that clip into a voice that says anything they type.
3. **They create panic.** The call is built around an emergency (an accident, an arrest, a kidnapping, a hospital bill) so you act before you think.
4. **They demand fast, untraceable money.** Wire transfers, gift cards, cryptocurrency, payment apps, or a "courier" who comes to collect cash.
5. **They isolate you.** "Don't tell Dad." "Don't hang up." "The lawyer said you can't talk to anyone." Secrecy keeps you from checking.

Variations target businesses too: a cloned "CEO" calls an employee and asks for an urgent wire transfer or gift cards.

## The warning signs

- **Extreme urgency.** Real emergencies rarely require payment within minutes.
- **Secrecy.** Any request to keep it from other family members is a huge red flag.
- **Strange payment methods.** Gift cards, crypto and wire transfers are scammer favorites because they're hard to reverse.
- **Short or evasive answers.** Cloned voices work best in short bursts. Scammers often hand the call to a "lawyer" or "officer" quickly.
- **A number you don't recognize**, or a caller ID that looks right but feels off. Caller ID can be spoofed.

## The family safe word: your best defense

Pick a **secret word or phrase** with your family, agreed in person and **never posted or texted**. Something random, not your pet's name or street.

If anyone calls claiming to be family in an emergency, ask for the word. A scammer with a perfect voice clone still won't know it. It takes two minutes to set up and works for grandparents, kids and everyone in between.

## What to do if you get the call

1. **Slow down.** The pressure is the scam.
2. **Ask for the safe word**, or a question only the real person would know.
3. **Hang up and call back** on the number you already have for them. Don't use a number the caller gives you.
4. **Check with someone else**: another family member, their partner, their school or workplace.
5. **Never pay** with gift cards, crypto or a wire transfer because of a call you can't verify.

## How to make your voice harder to clone

You can't hide completely, but you can reduce the raw material:

- Consider setting social media accounts with lots of talking videos to **private**.
- Use a **generic voicemail greeting** instead of recording your own voice.
- Be wary of unknown calls that try to keep you talking with odd questions.

## If you've been targeted

In the U.S., report it to the FTC at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov) and to the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center at [ic3.gov](https://www.ic3.gov). If you've already sent money, contact your bank or the payment service immediately. Acting fast sometimes stops the transfer.

## The bottom line

AI made voices easy to fake, but it didn't make *trust* easy to fake. Set up a family safe word this week, make "hang up and call back" a reflex, and share this with the people in your life most likely to get the call.
